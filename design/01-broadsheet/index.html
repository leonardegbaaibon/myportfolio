03Method

How I work, written plainly.

Four years of doing this has mostly taught me what to do first and what to leave alone.

I start on a real device

Simulators lie about performance, network behaviour and battery. I keep a mid-range Android in the loop from day one, because that is what most users in the markets I build for actually hold. The Kabu battery work and the NidiDrive load-time work both came from testing on the slow device rather than the fast one.

Offline is the default case

Every app above assumes the connection drops mid-transaction. That means idempotent writes, a reconciliation pass on resume, and never letting a spinner be the only state. On Kabu the ride state reconciles from SQLite when the app comes back.

I ship to the store, not to a branch

Provisioning profiles, review rejections, staged rollouts and phased release notes are part of the job. I have taken apps through Apple review and Play review enough times to plan around it rather than be surprised by it.

Design is not a separate phase

I work in Figma before I work in the editor, and I keep the token set in the repository so the two do not drift. Most of the retention gains I have measured came from reworking a flow, not from adding a feature.

Instrument before optimising

Sentry, crash grouping, and a small set of funnel events go in early. The 68% memory reduction on BlockEx was found in a heap snapshot, not guessed at. Without numbers, performance work is decoration.

I write the handover

Contracts end. When mine do, the team gets an architecture note, the deployment runbook, and the list of things I would do next in priority order. Talstrike V2 was scoped that way before I handed it on.
